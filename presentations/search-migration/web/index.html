Running Search

From PostgreSQL pg_trgm to Elasticsearch BM25

Amir Hossein Rassafi · May 2026 · amir-h-rassafi.github.io

The Story

What

pg_trgm, BM25, and tokenization.

Why

25M names, 15s queries, CPU saturation.

How

Replicated search index, analyzers, benchmarks, rollout.

Problem

  • Name search over almost 25M records.
  • PostgreSQL with pg_trgm handled fuzzy matching.
  • Some queries took around 15 seconds.
  • A few concurrent requests could saturate database CPU.

pg_trgm Intuition

Text similarity

Strings are sliced into 3-character fragments. Similar names share many fragments.

Example

search and serch share 4 fragments out of 9 unique fragments.

similarity = 4 / 9 = 0.44

BM25 Intuition

  • BM25 ranks documents by search relevance.
  • Rare terms carry more weight.
  • Repeated terms help with diminishing returns.
  • Short, focused documents are rewarded.
Key distinction: pg_trgm is similarity. BM25 is relevance scoring over tokens.

Migration Requirements

  • Deploy Elasticsearch quickly with low operational overhead.
  • Replicate required PostgreSQL data into Elasticsearch.
  • Preserve fuzzy, language-agnostic name search behavior.
  • Benchmark old and new ranking behavior.
  • Prove quality before production rollout.

Why BigQuery In The Middle?

  • Append-only change history for debugging.
  • Time partitioning helped control cost.
  • Every insert, update, and delete stayed inspectable.
  • It made consistency issues easier to investigate.

Lag-Aware Processing

  • Do not process the absolute latest timestamp.
  • Read from a small window in the past.
  • Use Datastream metadata and sequence ordering.
  • In this case, 15 minutes of freshness lag was acceptable.
Reason: newest visible data in BigQuery does not always mean the whole CDC pipeline has settled.

Closing

Elasticsearch did not replace pg_trgm directly.

It recreated the useful signals through analyzers, weighting, benchmarks, and a replayable indexing pipeline.
